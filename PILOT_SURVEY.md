# AscendX — Pilot Tester Survey

*For first-time testers, sent immediately after their first completed session (while the experience is still fresh). Keep it to ~5 minutes — completion rate drops fast past 15 questions. Mix of rating scales (fast to answer, easy to aggregate) and a few open-ended questions (where the real insight comes from).*

---

## Section 1 — Before AscendX (problem validation)

*This section is what makes the survey useful beyond product feedback — it's evidence the problem is real, independent of whether your specific product nailed it.*

1. Before today, how did you usually prepare for interviews? *(open text — or multi-select: mock interviews with friends/family, career services, YouTube/articles, other AI tools, didn't really prepare, other)*
2. On a scale of 1–5, how anxious or unprepared do you typically feel going into a real interview? *(1 = Very confident and prepared, 2 = Fairly confident, 3 = Somewhat anxious, 4 = Quite anxious, 5 = Very anxious or unprepared)*
3. Have you used any other interview-prep tool or app before (AI-based or not)? If yes, which, and what was missing? *(open text)*

## Section 2 — The session itself

4. How closely did the questions match what you'd actually expect for [target role]? *(1 = Nothing like a real interview for this role, 2 = A little off, 3 = Reasonably close, 4 = Very close, 5 = Exactly what I'd expect)*
5. How useful were the follow-up questions (probes) in getting you to give a better answer? *(1 = Not useful at all, 2 = Slightly useful, 3 = Moderately useful, 4 = Very useful, 5 = Extremely useful — pushed me to a noticeably better answer)*
6. Did the feedback feel specific to *your* answers, or generic? *(1 = Very generic, could apply to anyone, 2 = Mostly generic, 3 = A mix, 4 = Mostly specific to me, 5 = Very specific — clearly about what I actually said)*
7. Was there a moment in the session where you felt genuinely helped or "caught out" in a useful way? What happened? *(open text — this is often the most valuable answer on the whole survey)*
8. Was there anything that felt confusing, repetitive, or annoying? *(open text)*

## Section 3 — Perceived value

9. Compared to how you'd normally prepare, was this better, worse, or about the same? *(better / worse / about the same, + why)*
10. If you did this again before a real interview next week, do you think it would meaningfully change how you perform? *(1 = No difference at all, 2 = Slight difference, 3 = Some difference, 4 = Noticeable difference, 5 = Significant difference — I'd walk in meaningfully more prepared)*
11. Would you use AscendX again before your next real interview? *(yes / maybe / no)*
12. If AscendX were a paid product, roughly what would feel fair to pay — per month, or per interview cycle? *(open text — don't offer price anchors, let them name a number)*
13. How likely are you to recommend AscendX to a friend preparing for interviews? *(0 = Not at all likely, 5 = Neutral, 10 = Extremely likely — standard NPS scale, 0–10)*

## Section 4 — Wrap-up

14. What's the one thing that would make you use this again? *(open text)*
15. Anything else you want to tell us? *(open text, optional)*

---

## Notes on running this

- **Timing matters more than the questions.** Send this within minutes of session completion (in-app modal beats email — email response rates for a first-time tester with no relationship to you yet will be low).
- **Q7 and Q8 are the two questions to actually read carefully**, not just tally. Everything else is for aggregate numbers; these two are where you find out what to fix first and what to lead with in the pitch.
- **Q12 and Q13 are your business-plan evidence.** A cluster of real willingness-to-pay numbers and an NPS score, even from a small pilot (10–20 people), is far more credible in a business plan than "we believe there is demand."
- **Scoring Q13 (NPS):** 0–6 = Detractor, 7–8 = Passive, 9–10 = Promoter. NPS = %Promoters − %Detractors. Don't read the raw average — a "7" is not a good score in NPS terms even though it sounds like one.
- Don't ask everything at once if you can avoid it — if you can follow up 1–2 weeks later with "did you have a real interview yet?", that's the outcome-tracking check-in we scoped earlier, not this survey. Keep them separate: this one is about the *practice session*, that one is about *what happened after*.
