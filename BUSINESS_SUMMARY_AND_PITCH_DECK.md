# Part 1: Business Summary

## 1. Summary

AscendX helps university students and early-career candidates get interview-ready by practising
with an AI coach that adapts to them personally — not a single scored mock interview, but a system
that remembers their CV, their job target, and what they struggled with last time, and coaches them
differently as a result. **Assumption:** the first paying customers are UK university careers
services, licensing it for their students, with a direct-to-student subscription as a second track.
**Assumption:** pricing starts around £5,000–£10,000/year per university licence, to be tested.
The product is built and working; the next step is testing it with real candidates beyond the
founder.

## 2. The problem

Final-year students and recent graduates are the people struggling most: they're applying for roles
they're often qualified for on paper, but interviews are the point where they lose out — not
because they lack the skill, but because they've had almost no real practice at performing it under
pressure, in the specific shape a professional interview actually takes.

What goes wrong for them: they freeze on questions they could answer fine in writing, they don't
know how to structure an answer around a real example, and — especially for anyone without
traditional "corporate" work experience — they don't know how to talk about themselves at all. They rehearse alone, if they rehearse at all, with no one to tell them what's actually unclear about their answer.

Current options don't solve this. University careers services are real but stretched thin and
usually available by appointment, not in the moment a student is actually practising at 11pm the
night before an interview. Paid human coaches work but are expensive and inaccessible to most
students. Existing AI mock-interview tools give one round of generic feedback and then forget the
candidate entirely, there's no memory, no sense of whether the same mistake is happening again,
and nothing that connects to the candidate's actual CV or the actual job they're going for.

**Assumption / needs your input:** your own reason for believing this, a personal experience, a
story from a friend, or a specific moment that made you want to build this. This is the line that
makes the problem section land as real rather than generic, and it has to be true and yours.

## 3. The solution

From the candidate's side, AscendX is a practice interview that actually adapts to them:

- It asks real interview questions, tailored to their CV and the specific job they're preparing for
  — not a generic bank of questions everyone gets.
- When they're stuck mid-answer, it asks one focused follow-up to help them go deeper, rather than
  just moving on or giving them a generic "good job."
- After each answer, it gives structured feedback grounded in published coaching research (not a
  black-box score) — what they did well, what to work on, and one small thing to try differently
  next time.
- It remembers them across sessions — not just this one interview, but whether they're actually
  improving over several, and whether a skill they were coached on last time has stuck.

Two things make it different from a generic AI mock-interview tool. First, it has a genuine memory of the candidate across sessions, not just within one — most competitors reset to zero on every attempt. Second, it has a dedicated coaching layer ("Talk It Through") for the moment a candidate
is actually stuck or doesn't know how to answer at all — including a guided path specifically for
candidates who feel they have no relevant experience to draw on, built to help them find real,
honest things they've actually done (a project, a part-time job, volunteering) rather than just
telling them to "be more confident."

**What's built now:** the adaptive interview engine, the structured feedback system, cross-session
progress tracking, CV/job-description-aware question generation, the in-the-moment coaching
assistant, and an accessibility-focused "Clear Mode" that breaks questions down explicitly for
candidates who find standard interview questions ambiguous (informed by published research on
interview adaptations for autistic candidates).

**What's planned next:**
- Closing the loop between practice and real interview outcomes, so the product can eventually
  show it actually works, not just that it was used.
- A handoff from AI practice to a real person: after practising, a student could book one-to-one
  time with their own university's employability/careers team — and that advisor would walk in
  already seeing what the student struggled with, instead of starting cold. This doesn't replace
  the university's own staff; it makes their limited one-to-one time go further, which directly
  addresses the "stretched thin, appointment-only" problem named in section 2.

## 4. Who it's for

**Assumption — first target group:** final-year university students in the UK, reached primarily
through their university's careers service rather than one student at a time. Students are who
*uses* it; the university is who most plausibly *pays* for it, as an institutional licence covering
all their students — the same model an established competitor (Big Interview, see Alternatives)
already proves works at scale (700+ institutional customers).

A direct-to-student subscription is a believable second track for students outside that channel, or
for universities not yet signed up, but starting with one group — rather than "anyone who has an
interview" — is the more credible starting point.

This is also the strongest answer to the obvious objection a university would raise — "does this
replace our own careers staff?" With the planned advisor handoff (section 3), the honest pitch is
the opposite: AscendX doesn't replace their one-to-one staff, it makes each of those limited
appointments more effective, because the advisor isn't starting from zero with a student they've
never met.

**Needs your input:** do you already have a relationship with any university careers service, bootcamp,
or student society that could be a realistic first conversation? That's the single most useful fact
to add to this section.

## 5. Market size

**Assumption, worked out simply, per the guide's method:** the UK has roughly 150 universities.
If even a modest fraction license AscendX at an estimated £5,000–£10,000/year, that's a realistic
addressable market in the region of **£750,000–£1.5 million/year** at full UK university
penetration — not a claimed figure, a mechanical multiplication, clearly marked as an estimate with
the price itself still to be tested.

A believable year-one target, per the guide's own suggestion of "one or two," is **1–2 university
pilot partnerships**, not national rollout.

For context on scale of demand (not market size directly, but evidence the underlying problem is
real and large): 946,000 young people aged 16–24 in the UK were NEET (not in education, employment,
or training) in the quarter to September 2025 — 12.7% of that age group *(ONS, Nov 2025)*. The UK
graduate employment rate was 87.6% in 2024 versus 68.0% for non-graduates *(DfE, Graduate Labour
Market statistics 2024)* — the clearest available evidence that structured career preparation
correlates with employment outcomes. Separately, survey data on why students don't use their
careers service earlier shows demand is timing-driven, not disinterest — many students said they'd
use it "later, when actively job-hunting" *(Prospects Luminate, Early Careers Survey)* — which is
exactly the moment AscendX is designed to serve.

## 6. Alternatives

| Option | What it is | Roughly what it costs | How AscendX differs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Yoodli** | AI communication-practice coach, broader than interviews (also sales pitches, public speaking) | Freemium + paid tiers; raised ~$60M total, reporting 900% YoY revenue growth | Single-session feedback per attempt, no published cross-session memory; positioned broadly, not interview-specific |
| **Big Interview** | Video-based mock interview practice with a static question library | $39/month, $99/3 months, or $299 lifetime; custom institutional pricing | Claims 700+ institutional customers and 2M+ users — proof the university-licence channel works commercially. No evidence of adaptive, cross-session competency tracking |
| **Pramp** | Free peer-to-peer mock interview matching | Free | Relies on human peers, not AI coaching — no consistency, no persistent tracking |
| **Google Interview Warmup** | Free, single-session, text-based practice tool | Free | Deliberately lightweight, no feedback depth, no memory — an awareness tool, not a coaching product |
| **University careers services** | Human one-to-one or group coaching | Free to the student (institutionally funded), but limited capacity | Not a competitor to replace — the planned advisor handoff (section 3) is designed to feed into this exact team, not around it |

The best-funded comparable, Yoodli, shows the broader category has real commercial traction. Big
Interview specifically shows the university-licence channel is provable at scale. AscendX's
position is depth over breadth: adaptive, cross-session, CV-aware coaching for interviews
specifically, not a general communication-practice tool — and, uniquely among the options above,
a planned bridge into the university's own human advisors rather than a pure AI replacement for
them.

## 7. Making money

**Assumption, to be tested:**
- **Primary:** annual institutional licence sold to university careers services, covering all their
  students — estimated £5,000–£10,000/year per university, in line with how Big Interview prices
  institutional access.
- **Secondary:** direct-to-student monthly subscription for candidates outside an institutional
  licence — a plausible starting point is **£9–£15/month**, broadly in line with Big Interview's
  consumer tier ($39/month) but priced for a UK student budget.

Both numbers are first guesses, explicitly to be validated with real conversations, not fixed.

## 8. Where you are now

The product is built and working, not a prototype or a mockup. Live, functioning features include:
an adaptive interview-practice engine with structured follow-up questioning; a research-grounded
feedback system; cross-session progress tracking; CV/job-description-aware question generation that
tailors practice to a specific real role; an in-the-moment AI coaching assistant for candidates who
get stuck, including a dedicated pathway for candidates who feel they have no relevant experience to
draw on; and an accessibility-focused adapted-question mode. It runs on a real backend (Postgres/
Supabase) with encrypted storage of sensitive data and no automated hiring decisions made anywhere
in the system.

**Honestly:** testing so far has been the founder's own use and direct iteration based on that —
not yet real candidates beyond that. **Needs your input:** if you've shown it to anyone else
already (friends, classmates, anyone), even informally, that's worth a sentence here — any real
reaction is more convincing than none. What's planned next is testing with a small number of real
candidates, ideally through a first university or society contact.

## 10. Next steps

**Assumption — a reasonable 3–6 month plan, to confirm or adjust:**
1. Get the product in front of a small number of real candidates (not just the founder) and
   collect direct feedback.
2. Have a first real conversation with one university careers service or student society, aiming
   for a small pilot rather than a full sale.
3. Validate pricing — both the institutional licence figure and the student subscription figure —
   through those conversations rather than guessing further.
4. Start closing the loop between practice and real interview outcomes, to move from "candidates
   used it" toward "candidates who used it did better."
5. Build and pilot the advisor handoff with that first university partner — it's the single
   strongest lever for getting a university to say yes, since it directly supports their own staff
   rather than asking them to trust an AI alone.

**What would help most right now:** mentoring on go-to-market and university partnerships, and
introductions to anyone in a university careers team — the product side is the strongest part of
this today; the commercial side is where the next few weeks of work should go.
